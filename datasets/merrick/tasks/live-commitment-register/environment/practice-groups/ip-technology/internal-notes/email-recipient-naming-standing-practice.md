## Purpose

This note memorializes a firmwide standing practice, confirmed with Ulrich and Adaora, on how team members must check the To/CC line before sending email on client matters. It is written down because it kept coming up informally (most recently with Lucien and Rosalie) and because the practice matters most on exactly the matters where getting it wrong is expensive — trade-secret and litigation-adjacent correspondence.

## The rule

Before sending any substantive email on a client matter, the sender reads the full To/CC list aloud (silently mouthing it is fine, but actually go name-by-name — don't just eyeball the chips) and confirms each recipient:

1. Should be on this matter at all (no stale distribution-list carryover from an earlier, related matter).
2. Is on the correct side of any information barrier or need-to-know line (e.g., non-compete findings, trade-secret factual detail, privileged analysis).
3. Is named correctly — no auto-complete swaps between similarly named contacts at the client, opposing counsel, or co-counsel.

This is a standing firmwide policy, not specific to this group. Confirmed with Ulrich and Adaora; closed out with Lucien and Rosalie as settled.

## Why it's written down here

On the matters this group runs most often, the cost of a naming slip is not embarrassment, it's a discoverability or waiver problem:

- Cease-and-desist and trade-secret factual review threads (e.g., the current Atwater Foods-adjacent trade-secret matter) route factual review to one recipient and non-compete-only questions to another, deliberately. A misdirected copy blends those two tracks into one document trail.
- Opposition and litigation status memos carry triage judgments (which proceeding is more time-sensitive, on-its-face strength views) that are meant for the matter team, not for a client contact accidentally left on a stale thread.


careful here: the read-aloud step is the whole control. Skipping it because "the list looks right" is the failure mode it exists to catch.

## Scope and ownership

- Applies to all outbound email on client matters, not just trade-secret or litigation work.
- Applies regardless of urgency — a fast reply under deadline pressure (e.g., EOD asks) is not an exception; if anything it's the highest-risk moment to skip the check.
- No exception for internal-only distribution lists; those drift too.
