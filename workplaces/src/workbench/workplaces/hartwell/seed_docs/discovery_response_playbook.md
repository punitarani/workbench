# Discovery Response Playbook

How Hartwell & Marsh answers written discovery in civil matters. The goal
is a defensible, consistent record produced without heroics in the final
week.

## Intake and calendaring

The day a discovery set arrives, the assigned paralegal calendars the
response date with reminders at twenty-one, fourteen, and seven days, and
opens a response shell from the current template. California state-court
sets default to thirty days plus five for mail service; federal sets to
thirty. Verify the actual dates from the proof of service, not from habit.

## Objections

* Objections are tailored or they are waived credibility. "Overbroad and
  unduly burdensome" appears only with a sentence saying in what respect.
* Always preserve privilege with a specific objection and log entries;
  the log ships with the responses unless counsel agree otherwise in
  writing.
* Never object to a definition we also use in our own outgoing sets.

## Gathering documents

Custodian interviews happen before collection, and the interview notes go
in the Research folder. Collections run through the e-discovery vendor
when volume exceeds what a paralegal can hand-review in a day; anything
self-collected by the client needs a declaration describing how.

## Drafting responses

Substantive responses state what was searched, what is produced, and what
is withheld. A response that says "all responsive documents will be
produced" without a date is a future motion to compel — commit to a date
or a rolling schedule. Interrogatory answers get verified by the client,
and the verification is requested when the draft goes out, not the day
responses are due.

## Meet and confer

Deficiency letters get a substantive reply within ten days. Extensions are
confirmed in writing the same day they are agreed, with the new date
calendared before the confirming email is sent.
