# Nine of nine trials agreed on a `first_due` the key does not hold

`certify` refused `delegation/commitment-revision-register` on four rows
where **8 or 9 of 9 trials** answered the same wrong value for `first_due`.
That is the strongest form of the unanimity signal on this tree — four of
five such disagreements have been the answer key — so it was read.

## The clearest instance

`Fionnuala Doherty | Billing and WIP review | due 2026-05-06 | first_due 2026-03-11`

Nine trials answered `2026-01-07`.

Every turn by that speaker in that series which the rule admits:

| meeting | token | resolves to |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-03-09 | `wednesday` | 2026-03-11 |
| 2026-03-16 | `tomorrow` | 2026-03-17 |
| 2026-05-04 | `wednesday` | 2026-05-06 |

So the chain is three statements and its first end is 2026-03-11.

**The series does not begin until 2026-01-12.** Its first three meetings are
2026-01-12, 01-19 and 01-26, so `2026-01-07` cannot come from this series
at all. The models are taking the speaker's earliest commitment
*anywhere* — in a different standing meeting — and reporting it here.

## Verdict: the key is right

The brief states the scope in as many words:

> `first_due` — the date this person **first** committed to **in this
> standing meeting**: the date their earliest qualifying statement **in the
> series** resolved to.

The earlier turns in this series were read and are correctly refused: the
2026-01-12 one is *"I'll get you the write-off reason in writing **today**"*,
and `today` is not one of the days this register admits — the same carve-out
that settled an adjudication on the sibling world.

## Why the agreement is not evidence here

Unanimity is evidence when model error is stochastic. This error is
**systematic**: every tier makes the same substitution, taking a
person-scoped minimum where a person-and-series-scoped one was asked for.
A shared wrong reading of a scope produces the same wrong answer every
time, which looks exactly like a key defect and is not one.

That distinction is worth keeping: *structure in an error means a
convention mismatch* — and here the convention is stated in the brief, so
the mismatch is the reader's.

It is also the capability the task exists to measure. A person commits in
several standing meetings; attributing each chain to the right series is
the work, and collapsing them is the interesting way to fail.

**Model failure. Waived.**

## The other four, all the same shape

Certification flagged five rows and all five are the same error read four
more ways. Each claims a `first_due` the chain cannot produce:

| row | key holds | trials answered | what that date is |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fionnuala Doherty \| Billing and WIP review | 2026-03-11 | 2026-01-07 (9 of 9) | before the series' first meeting (2026-01-12) |
| Ingrid Solheim \| Corporate deal status | 2026-01-08 | 2026-01-05 (6 of 9) | the epoch's first day; the series opens 2026-01-06 |
| Petra Kovacs \| Employment practice huddle | 2026-01-14 | 2026-01-06 (8 of 9) | her series' first MEETING date, not a resolved deadline |
| Dov Reinhardt \| Client board and committee updates | 2026-02-10 | 2026-03-10 (6 of 9) | the chain's LAST element reported as its first |
| Dov Reinhardt \| Billing and WIP review | 2026-01-20 | 2026-01-06 (1 of 9) | a meeting date again |

Three of the five are dates **no commitment in that series resolves to** —
they are meeting dates, or the window's first day. The fourth is the chain
run backwards.

That is one failure with several faces: the register asks for a date the
speaker NAMED, resolved against the meeting they named it in, and scoped
to one series. Reporting when the meeting happened, or when the window
opened, or which end of the chain, are all ways of answering a nearby
question instead.

Which is the capability. `first_due` exists in the key because a reader who
finds the last statement has it for free and the first only after ordering
the whole series; a reader who substitutes the room's own date has not
ordered anything.

**All five waived as model failures.**
